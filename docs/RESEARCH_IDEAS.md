# Ideas pulled from GPT's research (2026-11-23)

Source: GPT's research dossier (19 games, 8 films and TV shows, 10 books, 6 museums and archives). The user pasted it in chat on 2026-11-23, and its source codes (S01-S52, H01-H06) are used here.

This is busy work while GPT reviews docs/LOOP_RUNDOWN.md: atmosphere and ideas, mapped onto what we already have. Every idea is a question with options to pick from. Claude's pick is marked ★ and the options mix freely.

The user's rule still holds: it's a game, you make it all, and 1000 men take the capital. The research gives us feeling and texture, not realism rules.

---

## 1. What sticks out (the short list)

1. **The things you make should remember.** Pacific Drive's garage ritual and This War of Mine's "supplies are someone's belongings" (S05, S17) fit our hand-made gear exactly. The tote rig with the patch you sewed is still on Ivo, worn at the edges, five ops later.
2. **Intel files are about people.** Orwell, The Lives of Others and the Ringelblum Archive (S24, S37, H04). Our files are currently just a tag and a price. A file could be a real letter, a photo or a payroll line, and sometimes you recognise the name: your foreman, a neighbour, one of your own.
3. **Every recruit joins for their own reason.** The Wind That Shakes the Barley, Land and Freedom, Battle Brothers backgrounds (S07, S34, S35): grief, money, pride, friendship, fear, belief. That gives 1000 men texture without a faction system.
4. **The job and the garage carry the weight.** Papers, Please, Andor, Red Rising (S25, S30, S45). The day job is more than a materials tap: the place, the people and your pride in the work.
5. **Distance only works if the interface is trusted.** Duskers worked; Radio Commander frustrated some players (S16, S19, S20). Our FIELD LINK faces and cut cams are right, but the reasons things happen must be readable: who saw what, why someone pushed back.
6. **Small acts first.** Alone in Berlin's postcards (S47) and Andor's ordinary people. Early in the game, a leaflet or a postcard is resistance too, and it is how followers start.
7. **The regime shows up as routines.** The Danish occupation's ration cards and blackouts (H03). Our "rule of the day" is a placeholder right now; it could be the whole texture of each day.
8. **A heist that grows into a revolution.** Mistborn (S39, S40) starts as a crew job and widens into politics. That fits our arc: garage → crew → cells → army.
9. **The autobattler needs variety and trust.** The Gladiator Guild Manager reviews (S14) complain of repetition and AI that sits still, so the op report should say why things happened.
10. **Something to come home to.** Frøslev's "life afterward" (H06) and Children of Men (S36): the ending, and the reason, is the ordinary life you want back.

## 2. Ideas by part of the game, as questions with options

### A. Gear that remembers (benches, the cage, FIELD LINK)
**Q-A1. What does an item remember?**
- a) ★ Who made it, the day, its stars, and every op it went on ("Tote rig ★★, made day 3, 4 ops, worn by Ivo").
- b) Marks you can see: patches, tape, scorch, a name written on it. Repairs add marks rather than resetting them.
- c) Kills and saves (a rig that carried the meds that saved Ada).
- d) Hidden: the item gets a nickname after 5 ops ("Lucky", "The Bag").
- e) Nothing visible; only the op report mentions it.

**Q-A2. Repair versus make new?**
- a) ★ Gear wears with use (condition %). The bench can REPAIR it: a short hands-on step (patch, re-tape, re-solder) that keeps the item's history, or you make a new one.
- b) Gear never wears.
- c) Wear only from the hits it takes in fights.
- d) Repairs can also upgrade (sew a better pouch on): the item grows instead of being replaced.

**Q-A3. Upgrades keep the old look ("better gear still shows its past")?**
- a) ★ Yes: an upgraded tote rig keeps its blue stripe under new straps.
- b) Upgrades replace the model.
- c) The player chooses (keep the look, like transmog).

### B. Intel that is about people (hacking, the Dead Drop, the corkboard)
**Q-B1. What does a file look like when you open it?**
- a) ★ A real document: a letter, a memo, a ledger page, a photo with a caption, an ID card. Readable and short; the user writes some, Claude generates the rest from the town.
- b) A card with a title and one line (what we have now).
- c) A Papers, Please style document with stamps and details you can check against other files.
- d) An Orwell-style profile that fills in with every file you find about a person.

**Q-B2. Do you recognise people in files?**
- a) ★ Yes: the town's people are shared. Your foreman, the neighbour who asked about the lights, a recruit's brother can all show up, and the file says so.
- b) Only story characters recur.
- c) No, all strangers.

**Q-B3. Choices about a file beyond sell or post?**
- a) Burn it: protect the person in it (loyalty and mood go up, no money).
- b) ★ Use it: dirt gives leverage on that person (a guard who looks away, a clerk who forges a pass).
- c) Give it to them: warn someone they're on the informant list, and they may join you.
- d) Keep it for an op (a "plan" file reveals a layout).
- e) All of the above as four buttons on the file.

### C. People with reasons (recruits, the cage, loyalty, the 1000)
**Q-C1. What drives a recruit?**
- a) ★ One motive each: grief, money, pride, family, faith or belief, friendship, revenge, boredom or thrill. It changes what they say, what they refuse and what keeps them loyal (money people want pay, grief people want the op against whoever hurt them).
- b) A background (old job), as in Battle Brothers: an ex-mechanic repairs, an ex-nurse heals, an ex-cop knows the precinct.
- c) Both: a background plus a motive.
- d) Traits only (we have steady, cocky, jumpy).

**Q-C2. How much of a person do we show at 1000 men?**
- a) ★ Named operators (about 12) get the full face, motive, background and history. Cell leaders (about 30-50) get a face and a motive. The militia are numbers, with a face sometimes surfacing in a ledger line ("Marek's sister joined the Tannery cell").
- b) Everyone has a face, procedurally generated.
- c) Only the named ones exist as people.

**Q-C3. Injuries (Battle Brothers: unpredictable loss divides players)?**
- a) ★ Injuries show on the face (a scar, a bandage, an eye patch) and heal over days. Permanent scars are kept. Death only when you let it happen: a clear warning, a retreat call, meds.
- b) Permadeath, any time, hard mode.
- c) No permanent effects.

### D. The garage between nights (morale, humour, attachment)
**Q-D1. Garage moments?**
- a) ★ After an op, or when you go to bed, a short line or scene with whoever is in the garage: someone fixed the radio, someone burned the coffee, two of them argue about a call you made. Pride and All Quiet in the Trenches energy.
- b) People physically visible in the garage, sitting at benches and cleaning guns (a 3D presence, more work).
- c) A photo wall that fills up (the polaroid on the desk becomes a wall of your people).
- d) Only text in the ledger.

**Q-D2. What do your people enjoy (things to give them)?**
- a) Cigarettes, vodka and good coffee as morale items. These are materials we already have.
- b) A radio playing banned music (a garage item you build or buy).
- c) Pairs who fight better together (XCOM 2 bonds).
- d) ★ a+c.

### E. The day job carries weight (economy, job cards)
**Q-E1. More from the job than materials?**
- a) ★ Coworkers: one per job is recruitable over time (they notice what you take).
- b) The workplace becomes an op target later (sabotage the mill or the hospital pharmacy).
- c) A shift as a quick minigame: stealing is hands-on (Papers, Please speed), not a menu choice.
- d) The job shapes what you notice: hospital workers see Bureau patients, the steel mill sees army orders.
- e) All of these over the course of the game.

### F. The regime in daily routines (time, the curfew map, the markets)
**Q-F1. Make "the rule of the day" real?**
- a) ★ Yes. Each day has one rule that changes play: curfew earlier (the dash is harder), a blackout (darker map, the PC is off), ration week (food costs more), a checkpoint (one street blocked), an electronics crackdown (ShopNet logs everything), the Marshal's birthday (Nod posts get less attention).
- b) A weekly rule instead of daily.
- c) Rules follow your attention ladder (the more they look, the harsher the rules).
- d) a+c.

**Q-F2. Ration cards?**
- a) ★ A material: you get a few a week, use them for food (upkeep in kind) or trade them at the Tram Yard. Forged ones from the forgery desk (an unused station we have).
- b) Flavour only.

### G. Small acts first (followers, early game)
**Q-G1. Early, low-risk resistance before guns?**
- a) ★ Leaflets and postcards: print them on the PC, drop them on the curfew dash. Followers and mood go up at low risk. The "Alone in Berlin" opening.
- b) Graffiti on the curfew map (a tag spot = a quick risk).
- c) Sabotage at work (Simple Sabotage style): slow the line, lose papers.
- d) All of these as the early-game "ops" before the bar raid.

### H. Distance and trust in FIELD LINK (combat)
**Q-H1. How do we make the auto-battle readable and trusted?**
- a) ★ A radio log down the side, with short spoken-style lines ("Ada: contact left", "Viktor: no, too hot"), plus icons on the board for what each person can see.
- b) An op report with a cause for every big event ("Ivo was hit because the door was lit; you called Go").
- c) Replays of key moments.
- d) a+b.

**Q-H2. Variety so ops don't repeat?**
- a) ★ Every op has a twist card (a guard dog, a power cut, a hostage, an informant on your side, rain).
- b) Generated buildings.
- c) Enemy types per place (watchmen, police, army, Bureau agents).
- d) All.

### I. Propaganda: a person versus their public symbol (Nod, followers)
**Q-I1. Filmed ops and symbols?**
- a) ★ A helmet-cam clip from an op can be posted on Nod (a big follower gain), but the fighter in it becomes a public face: more Bureau heat on them and more recruits drawn to them.
- b) Posters you make (a bench recipe: propaganda).
- c) You stay anonymous; only the movement's name grows.

### J. Arc and ending
**Q-J1. Story shape?**
- a) ★ Mistborn shape: act 1 is crew heists (the garage), act 2 is cells and the town, act 3 is the army and the capital.
- b) Open sandbox the whole way.

**Q-J2. Ending?**
- a) ★ Life afterward: an epilogue per named person, and you at the dinner table with Hana. What did it cost, who came home.
- b) The capital falls and the credits roll.
- c) Several endings by how you won (strike, defection, storm).

## 3. Funds and materials: when and where you use them

The user asked how funds and materials are earned and used over a day and a week.

| When | Get | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | steal from work (materials), ration cards (weekly, if Q-F2) | |
| Evening, free | PC: sell or post intel; ShopNet and Lantern orders | buy materials, tools, ammo; pay for intel tools |
| Evening, 1 action each | hack (files, crates, PAYDAY), long crafts, the curfew trip (the Tram Yard: barter, buy guns) | craft gear; buy the good grades |
| After an op | loot, money (if the op pays), freed people | ammo, meds and gear spent |
| Night | wages; parcels and crates arrive | upkeep per person; bribes on payroll (if Q8 in the rundown) |
| Week | a need: rent, Hana's insulin, a cell asking for meds or gear | money or the item itself |
| Campaign | districts and production lines (the rundown's Q2/Q3) | gear for hundreds, safe houses, vehicles |

**Q-M1. What should money feel like?**
- a) ★ Always a little short: a weekly need that competes with gear, so every Tram Yard trip is a choice.
- b) Plentiful; materials are the bottleneck.
- c) Tight early, abundant late; the bottleneck moves to people.

**Q-M2. Can materials pay for things?**
- a) ★ Yes, at the Tram Yard and with people (pay a recruit in cigarettes, a guard in vodka).
- b) Only credits.
