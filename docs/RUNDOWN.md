5. **Consequences.** The hunt is designed but barely wired in: clues land on the corkboard and nothing else happens. The Bureau needs to react (raids, checkpoints, the inspector closing in).
6. **The world.** No map, no news, no sense of a country moving. Section 5.

## 5. The world map (user, 2026-11-22: "I don't want it simple")
- **Zoom levels:** the country (cities), then a city (zones), then a zone (buildings). It's in the war room, and HEARTH shows the state's version.
- **Control is building by building.** A zone flips when you hold its key buildings; a city flips when its zones do; then the next city.
- **News:** the state's spin plus what really happened, so your ops show up, lied about. A ticker on the map; StateNet's front page.
- **A living world:** the regime moves without you (patrols, crackdowns, propaganda pushes, other cells rising and falling, prices changing), shown as map changes and news. You don't watch it happen; you see that it did.

## 7. Decided (user, 2026-11-22)
1. **The opening:** inherit Pavel's half-built operation: his laptop, the half-finished box on the desk (your first craft finishes it), a bench under a tarp, lantern's first message. Three to five light beats, tutorials in disguise.
2. **The clock:** nights, but only big things cost. A few actions a night (an op, a job, a long craft); small crafting, the PC and the box are free; the world turns over every night (news, the Bureau, the map).
3. **People:** recruited from your world (contacts, Nod followers, the rescued, people turned with dirt). Each has a name, one line and a face. They level by surviving, trainers raise doctrine, old ones retire into trainers or cell leaders, and the dead stay dead.
4. **The leader dies or is caught:** roguelite. A successor takes over; you lose a lot (gear seized, a safehouse burned, contacts spooked), but base progress stays.
5. **Top-end weapons:** select-fire rifles, a light machine gun, a marksman rifle, plus mortars and drones. Rare, hard to build (the mill, good parts). Early game: bolt actions, a pump shotgun, pistols.
6. **Money:** intel and loot pay (the market behind the market, client jobs, stolen fuel cards and payroll, your people's day jobs); it buys materials, tools, rounds, bribes and safehouses. Your cover job pays a little.
7. **Map size:** three or four generated cities, then the capital. The home city is taken zone by zone, building by building.
8. **When the Bureau finds you:** a warning (a car outside, a neighbour's tip), then a raid you defend at home with your people, in the same fight engine: run, hide the gear, or fight. Losing costs a safehouse and gear.
