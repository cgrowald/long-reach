# Literature like Red Dawn: comparisons and gaps (2026-11-23)

User ask: find the books, films and history in the Red Dawn vein and compare them to our game. Where does stuff not make sense?
How do they trade what they have for better stuff? Who talks about logistics?

This doc covers the themes and design lessons only. It contains no how-to of any kind.

Left out on purpose: extremist "manual" novels written as recruitment (The Turner Diaries and its imitators).
They are propaganda, not stories about how people survive, and nothing in them helps a game about a parent protecting a family.

### History (the logistics is here, not in the novels)
| Work | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Defiance**, Nechama Tec (Bielski partisans) | Food was the biggest problem every day. Food squads took from farmers, ideally only those with spare, and sometimes by force. A camp workshop repaired guns and built new ones from spare parts. The camp **traded services** (repair, tailoring, leather) to other partisan units **for food and arms** |
| **Rising '44**, Norman Davies; Home Army histories | The Home Army **bought guns from German soldiers and on the black market**, captured more, and built its own submachine gun (Błyskawica) with parts **made in about 20 small workshops** and final assembly in a church cellar. The uprising then failed without outside help |
| **Blue Helmets and Black Markets**, Peter Andreas (siege of Sarajevo) | Cash was worthless, so trade ran on barter and hard currency. **Cigarettes were money** (tunnel workers were paid a pack a day). One tunnel under the airport moved food, currency and arms. Front lines made prices: what was cheap on one side was gold on the other, and **prices jumped when a route closed** |
| Michael Collins and the Irish War of Independence (e.g. Tim Pat Coogan, "Michael Collins") | **Intelligence first**: Collins had friendly clerks inside the post office and police offices reading the enemy's mail. The Easter Rising (1916) was centred on the **General Post Office**. A real basis for our tutorial hack |
| **Seven Pillars of Wisdom**, T. E. Lawrence | Raid supply lines, never hold ground, live on mobility; camels and water are the real constraint |
| Mao, "On Guerrilla Warfare"; Che Guevara, "Guerrilla Warfare" | The phases: survive and organise, then small hits, then open war. "The enemy is our quartermaster": most guns come from the enemy. The people are the water the guerrilla swims in |
| Kochanski, "Resistance: The Underground War in Europe" | Escape lines, the underground press, outside supply drops. Shows how much of resistance was printing and carrying, not shooting |
| OSS "Simple Sabotage Field Manual" (1944, declassified) | Ordinary workers slowing the enemy from inside: be slow, lose papers, misroute things. Our day-job steal choice already lives here |

## 2. How they get better stuff (the trade ladder)

Every one of these stories climbs the same ladder:
1. **What you already own.** Hunting rifles, farm tools, kitchen chemistry, the family car (Red Dawn, Tomorrow).
2. **Take it from work or neighbours.** Pilfering, food squads, borrowing (Bielski, Simple Sabotage, our steal choice).
3. **Trade a skill.** The Bielski workshops fixed other units' guns for food and arms. A doctor, a tailor or a radio repairman is worth more than money.
4. **Buy from the enemy's corrupt people.** The Home Army bought from German soldiers. Sarajevo's black market ran partly through the peacekeepers. Harry Lime is the dark version.
5. **Capture it.** Mao and Lawrence: the enemy's depot is your armoury. Every successful raid should arm the next one.
6. **Outside help.** Allied supply drops, the Soviet "gate" corridor, foreign money. Warsaw is what happens when it doesn't come.
7. **Make it yourselves, spread out.** The Błyskawica: many small shops each make one harmless-looking part, one hidden room assembles.

**Our game today:** steps 1, 2 and 7 (one garage), and part of 4 (the fixer buys our files). Missing:
- **Step 3: trading skills and services** for goods.
- **Step 4: corrupt enemy sellers** at the black market, with the risk that one is a sting.
- **Step 5: captured gear** coming home from raids. The op report should hand back what the squad picked up.
- **Step 6: outside help** as a late-game route.
- **Step 7 spread out:** the garage is one point of failure. Later, farm parts out to contacts so no one place is damning.

## 3. Where our game doesn't make sense yet (gaps)

Each gap lists what the works do, what we do, and the fix. Ordered by how soon it bites.

1. **Money has nowhere to go.** In every story scarcity drives the plot and money is spent constantly.
   - Fix: the three markets being built now (section 4).
2. **One currency.** Sarajevo ran on cigarettes, hard currency and favours, never the local cash.
   - Fix: the curfew market takes **barter**. Your stolen materials, meds and smokes are money there, and some sellers refuse credits.
3. **Prices never move.**
   - Fix: prices react to the Bureau. A closed checkpoint or a crackdown pushes black market prices up; a big raid floods the market with stolen goods and prices drop.
4. **Food and upkeep are abstract.** Bielski's whole day was food.
   - Our upkeep is 8 cr per person. That is fine for now, but later upkeep should be *things* (food, meds, cigarettes) once the group grows past the garage.
5. **The town doesn't pay for what we do.** Red Dawn, Tomorrow and Army of Shadows all turn on **reprisals**: the occupier punishes the town.
   - Fix: big hits raise a town-wide crackdown (curfew earlier, checkpoints, a neighbour taken). Support can swing either way: anger at us, or anger at them.
6. **No popular support stat.** Mao's "water" and Little Brother's recruits are the same thing: the people.
   - Our Nod followers are half of it.
   - Fix: a town **mood** that followers, reprisals and propaganda move; it sets recruit quality and how many informants there are.
7. **Who knows what.** Algiers and Army of Shadows: a captured member gives up names.
   - Fix: we need a small "knows" graph (who has seen the garage, who knows whom). A captured fighter or a fired co-worker can expose what they know. Cells and handles are how you limit it.
8. **The black market is safe.** In The Third Man, Sarajevo and Andor it is dangerous and dishonest.
   - Fix: fakes and bad grades (watered meds), sellers who skim, and the occasional sting.
9. **Guns come from the bench only.** Historically most came from the enemy.
   - Fix: raid loot, buying from a crooked guard, and building as the slow fallback.
10. **Fighters learn instantly** (Red Dawn's worst sin). Marsden's teens break down; Kessel's people burn out.
    - Fix: skill comes from ops survived, and stress/trauma from ops lost. Already partly in loyalty; stress is a later system.
11. **Rising too early loses** (Warsaw 1944).
    - Fix: storming the capital should need conditions you build: town mood, army defectors, outside help, a stockpile. Without them it is a brave, doomed fight.
12. **Information is the first weapon** (Collins, Moon, Little Brother).
    - We already lead with hacking. The tutorial at the post office follows Collins directly: read the enemy's mail first.

## 4. Decisions this feeds (built 2026-11-23)

- **Three markets:**
  - **ShopNet** (legal, online): basic hardware only, every purchase is logged with your ID, and big electronics orders raise Bureau attention.
  - **Lantern** (black, on the PC): a short, pricey list paid in credits and delivered to a dead drop, with some scams.
  - **The curfew market** (in person, after 22:00): the most stock and the best grades. It takes barter and credits, and corrupt sellers sit next to stings. You reach it through the sneak dash.
- **Lockpicking** pin by pin.
- **Tutorial:** the post office first (Collins and the GPO), then the town hall.
