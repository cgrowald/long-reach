# Core loop (consolidated, 2026-11-21)

*Back to the drawing board. This sheet pulls together every Running Thought (2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21) and the docs into one loop, and names where the ideas pull against each other. Nothing here is built. The user decides the conflicts in section 5, then we build the smallest playable slice of section 6.*

---

## 1. The one idea that ties it all together

**Every way of getting what you need is the same kind of game: a "job".**

You said it on day one: *every need has several ways to meet it*. For meds, that's make them, steal them, work at the hospital, or exploit a hospital worker. Today you said the hacking cards "roll into exploiting a person: getting codes, dirt or intel out of someone." So one engine, many skins:

| Path | The "map" you work through | Your cards come from | Example chain (the combo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hack** | The target's network (firewall → shell → database → vault) | Techniques learned + gadgets made at the hacking bench | scan → access → exploit → payload |
| **Exploit a person** | The person (guarded → chatty → trusting → leveraged) | Dirt you hold, things you know about them, favours, charm | find their weak spot → rapport → leverage → the ask |
| **Break in** | The building (door → alarm → camera → safe) | Lockpicks, keycode cracker, cloned badge, crowbar | case it → get in → kill the alarm → grab |
| **Work there** (cover job) | The shift (clock in → access → the moment → walk out) | Your job, your uniform, your reputation | blend in → be trusted → be alone → take it |
| **Later: send people** | The op (approach → breach → clear → extract) | Your soldiers and their star-rated gear | watch-and-decide, or auto-resolve on stars |

**Shared rules for every job:**
- **Score sets the quality of the loot.** Bad: the building. Great: the crate photo and the buyer.
- **Each card is used once per job.**
- **Combos matter:** the "right" order is the real-world logic of that path, gamified. That's the "OHHH, that's how you do it" moment.
- **Your tools have star ratings** that act like Balatro's jokers. They make every job faster, and the numbers bigger, as you grow.
- **Heat (the trace) fills as you work.** Push your luck or pull out.

This answers "hacking is just a small part": it's one path of four, and you lean into whichever you like (lean-in, decided 2026-11-20). A hacker run happens because you hacked a lot.

## 2. The loop

> **A need → pick a target and a path → prep (make or buy cards at the benches) → play the job → loot (scored) → the benches turn loot + parts into gear and new cards → better tools, bigger targets → people join → you outfit them → you send them → you call the big targets.**

- **A day (Papers Please + Iron Nest):**
  1. The morning rule (it changes today's jobs) and your messages: needs, offers, warnings.
  2. You have **a few hours** after your cover job. Each job or bench session costs hours, so you pick 2–3 things a night.
  3. The minigames reset every day. Same verbs, new conditions.
  4. Night: heat settles, the Bureau reacts, sleep saves.
- **The arc (Antistasi):**
  - Alone in the garage → a few trusted people → cells → squads → the war → the capital.
  - Or impeachment through one perfect evidence drop.
  - The town map turns your colour place by place. The regime escalates.
- **Three progressions that feed each other (decided 2026-11-20):**
  - Your skills create your gear.
  - Your gear upgrades your people.
  - Knowing people teaches you new things and speeds up bench work (which later gets automated).

## 3. What's kept from before (still true)
- **World:** a generated country and town; 1984 with sloppy new tech; flaws everywhere; a state OS full of backdoors.
- **The Iron Nest room:** the garage, the PC, separate labelled benches (you buy kits to open them), physical tools, things you touch.
- **Look:** low-poly and pixelated (White Knuckle), animations carry it.
- **Money** is the only currency. Parts are items. Loot is posted, sold or used. It never upgrades soldiers directly; it goes through the benches.
- **Early money:** the cover job, repair side jobs, and selling dirt and intel to groups.
- **Heat:**
  - Bureau warnings in levels, ending in raids. A raid is a scramble to destroy or move SOME of your stuff, or all of it if you're fast.
  - Heat comes down by lying low, bribes, moving, or framing someone.
- **Prison:** early prison with no one to free you = game over. Later you earn ways out.
- **Recruits:**
  - from your circle, followers and people you rescue;
  - inner-circle operators on 3-day loans ("rented FIFA cards");
  - everyone has a family and remembers.
- **Named legends:** a few you care about. Star gear makes them survive. Everyone else is numbers.
- **Groups:** reporters, arms people and hackers buy what you find. Later they become Tarkov-style traders who pull against each other.
- **Nod** (state social media): post dirt, gain followers (recruits, money, crowd protection, inner-circle invites), draw the Bureau.
- **Story:** your stories set the opening and fire when the systems get you there. Pavel is one side story.
- **Tone:** a bleak regime, a darkly funny own side. Only adults are hurt.

## 4. What changed with the restart
- The open PC with many apps (Market, Workshop, Nod, Map) was **buttons, not a game**. Apps stay as *places* in the OS, but every *action* is a job minigame or a bench minigame.
- **Hacking moved from typed commands to cards and combos.** (You love typing: see conflict C.)
- **New look,** and the old build is tagged `v1-final`.

## 5. Where your ideas pull against each other (decide these)
**A. Combat: which one?** Three versions are in your notes:
1. **Watch-and-decide real time** (stack on the door, the breach plan, drone view, pie the corners). Built as a prototype, and you liked the doctrine feel.
2. **XCOM send-away:** pick people, they're gone for days, you get a report.
3. **A Balatro or Ball x Pit fight at the end of the day.**

*Claude's take:* (2) for routine jobs your people do, and (1) for the big ops you choose to watch, using the same squad and the same stars. Drop (3) unless it's the auto-resolve readout.

**B. Going out: how big?**
- *Shadows of Doubt* (a first-person, generated town with real interiors) is your dream, but it's the most expensive thing in the whole game.
- Your earlier answer was "town map, click a place, a short hands-on scene, back".

*Claude's take:* the town map plus the **job minigame as the scene**: the break-in job happens *in* a small low-poly room you look at. Grow toward Shadows of Doubt later.

**C. Typing.** You loved the typing (memorising the login pattern, getting past two-factor) but chose cards. *Claude's take:* keep both. Typed "flourishes" at key moments (a typed password you learned grants a bonus card), and cards for the rest.

**D. "Laptop upgrades are out" vs "your phone or computer decides what you can exploit and how you command".** *Claude's take:* no specs. **Rig modules with stars** are your upgrades, and they're fun, not chores.

**E. Pressure: why does tonight matter?** Still undecided, and it's the most important gap. Options:
- a weekly bill (rent, family);
- a need with a deadline (Hana's insulin by Friday);
- Bureau heat;
- the movement's demand ("the cell needs 3 rifles for the depot hit Sunday").

*Claude's take:* needs with deadlines that come from your people and family. They're personal, they vary, and they point you at a target.

**F. Benches: gear for you, or for your people?** Decided: both. Hand-made for you first, automated into "standard issue" later with machines and workers that cost money.

## 5b. Decided (user, 2026-11-21)
- **A. Combat:** yes, exactly. Routine jobs your people do are XCOM-style send-aways. The big ops you choose to watch are watch-and-decide. Same squad, same stars.
- **B. Going out:** walled off with placeholder text until we build it. It doesn't need to be as deep as Shadows of Doubt.
- **C. Typing:** yes, keep both. That's the Iron Nest feel: you're *in* the world, but this world's hacking tools are minigames, not boring scripts.
- **D. Upgrades:** rig modules are **built at the bench**. The good stuff is **illegal tech you can't just buy**. Getting it is why you join the insurgency network and grow your cell, which eventually becomes the biggest one.
- **E. Pressure:** **the state's "gestapo" (the Bureau) coming to find you.**
  - The player should want this game because they want to overthrow the government.
  - Every choice you make brings you closer to getting caught, and there are mechanics to get away from that.
  - Think of the cure meter in Plague Inc., but as a **government detective hunting you**, and deeper than a single meter.
  - Family needs come later, as extra pressure, not the main one.
- **F. Benches:** both (already decided).

**Next design job: the hunt.** What does the detective actually do (evidence, leads, trails, suspects, warrants)? What do you do back (cover tracks, misdirect, frame, bribe, move, lie low)? How does each job's trace feed it? It has to be readable (always warned, never a random death) and richer than one meter. Research: Plague Inc.'s cure, the Nemesis system, Payday's heat, Shadows of Doubt's investigations, Hitman's notoriety, Mount & Blade's manhunts.

## 6. The smallest slice that proves the loop (only once A to F are settled)
One week. One need with a deadline. Two paths to meet it (hack, or exploit a person) on the same job engine. One bench that makes cards. Loot scored by tier. Heat. A night ledger. Placeholder everything else.

If that week is fun, the rest is content.
