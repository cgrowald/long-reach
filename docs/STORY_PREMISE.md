# Story premise (for the user to write from)

*Claude's job: the premise, the cast, the beats and the scene list. The user's job: writing the scenes. Change anything here; it's a starting point, not canon. Where the text goes and how to edit it: `docs/WRITING_GUIDE.md`.*

---

## The premise in one paragraph

Valdenmark, 2041. The Republic runs on screens: digital IDs, face-matching cameras, a citizen app that pays you in points for reporting your neighbors. It all looks like the future, but it was built by the lowest bidder and maintained by nobody. Tomas Halden is a truck mechanic who has never broken a rule he could avoid. Three months ago his brother Pavel went through the Oster bridge checkpoint and the Republic decided he never existed. When Pavel's things come back in a cardboard box, Tomas finds a phone number and the word *lantern*, and a laptop that used to belong to a woman who also doesn't exist anymore. He isn't brave. He just can't stop.

**The central question:** how much of your family's safety will you spend on your brother, and on strangers, and who do you become doing it?

---

## The world in story terms

- **The Marshal**, Iosif Darvan, took power on the Night of Renewal (2030). He's everywhere on screens, nowhere in person. Vain, paranoid, absurd: and the people who disappear are real.
- **The machine is stupid; the people running it are not all stupid.** The cameras miss things. The clerks don't. Fear does most of the work.
- **"There is no record."** The regime's favorite weapon is erasure. The resistance's favorite weapon is proof: a photo, a ledger, a name read out loud.
- **Everyone has a reason to look away:** a job, a kid, a pension, points on the app.

## How stories work in the game (decided 2026-11-20)

- **Pavel's arc is an opening story,** not the whole game. Its job is to give you access (contacts, the forum, proof, your first people) so the insurgency can start. It doesn't have to go too hard.
- **The ending of this arc** (one possible version): you win, and you find Pavel. A scene where you can say that everything you left behind led here. You hand him the leadership, and soon you both give it up for **a ring of trusted leaders** you met along the way.
- **No single correct answer.** Every story has several outcomes depending on what you did: Pavel alive or dead, Viktor friend or enemy, Mira with you or gone.
- **Many stories:**
  - the user writes about **10 opening stories** (one per "what was taken from you" in character creation, or more);
  - once there are enough, Claude writes many more in the user's style, so the world feels open and new every run;
  - all of it is written during development and stored as data. **The game itself doesn't run an AI.**

## The first campaign's arc (the 10-hour proof of concept)

1. **The box** (week 1, T1 covers Monday-Thursday): Pavel's things, lantern, the forum, Station 4. Ends with: *he may be alive.*
2. **The reading group** (weeks 2-3): first recruits, first propaganda, first informant. Viktor gets closer. Mira finds out (or doesn't). Ends with: *Leo is not who he says.*
3. **Proof** (weeks 4-6): break into the Kessel Annex for the full ledger; take down the cameras on Grenz Road. The city sees the list. Ends with: *the regime answers, and someone pays.*
4. **Station 4** (weeks 7-9): the plan to get Pavel out. What it costs: Viktor's keys, Koval's silence, a life. Ends with: *Pavel comes home, or doesn't, and Kessel rises either way.*
5. **The first province** (the proof of concept's ending): the movement is real now. You can't go back to the plant.

## Scenes to write (pick any)

Each is short: 100-400 words, the way the day cards read now. Write them in any text editor; Claude puts them into the game.

1. **Tuesday dinner, rewritten.** Lena asks about Uncle Pavel. Make the table feel like a real family: Agnes's radio, Mira's day at the hospital, the peas.
2. **The reading group** (Friday). Six people in Hana's basement. It's illegal since this morning. Who's there, what they read, who talks too much, who stays quiet (Leo).
3. **Viktor's beers** (Saturday). Two old friends on the back step. Viktor warns Tomas about "the file going around the station", out of love.
4. **Lena's birthday** (Sunday). No choices. Just the reason Tomas is doing this. Pavel's empty chair.
5. **A forum thread in someone else's voice.** ferry_woman, moth or kettle posting about something that happened in their district. Darkly funny or devastating, your call.
6. **A Ministry announcement.** Pure regime voice: cheerful, bureaucratic, threatening without noticing. (A new rule, or the Marshal's birthday week.)
7. **Mira finds out.** However you think it happens.
